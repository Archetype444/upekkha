# Instructions

This app helps you perform and track your progress of the maximum pause exercise as taught by [Learn Buteyko Online](https://learnbuteyko.substack.com/).

The maximum pause exercise helps you increase your tolerance to carbon dioxide with a combination of breath holding and shallow breathing.

- Shallow breathing helps break your habit of over breathing and builds a store of CO2, this [helps your body deliver oxygen to cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohr_effect).
- Breath holding trains your respiratory system to tolerate a higher threshold of CO2.

Despite its apparent simplicity, the Buteyko Method is infamously difficult to learn without a teacher. I tried for several months and made very little progress until I took a course and got some coaching.

Please note there are [contraindications for maximum pause exercises](https://www.normalbreathing.com/breath-holds/).

## Overview

To make steady progress, you should:

- Perform this exercise four times a day.
- Practice should be on an empty stomach (two hours after a meal). A good schedule is to practice before breakfast, lunch, dinner, and bed.
- Sessions should be at least two hours apart.
- Only breathe into your belly (not chest)
- Only breathe through your nose (not mouth).
- During maximum and control pauses:
  - Pinch your nose shut (this allows your breathing muscles to relax).
  - Relax as much as possible.
  - Avoid watching the timer.

## Pulse

At the beginning and end of each session, you measure and record your pulse.

Your pulse should be lower at the end than at the beginning. If it isn't, wait 5-15 minutes and check again. If your pulse is still higher, this can be an indication that you are performing the exercise incorrectly or over training. Please get advice from an experienced teacher.

## Control Pause

Your control pause (sometimes called a [BOLT score](https://oxygenadvantage.com/measure-bolt/)) is a way to measure your current CO2 tolerance and the Buteyko Method’s primary metric to track progress.

1. Sit quietly for a few minutes.
2. After a normal inhale and exhale, pinch your nose closed.
3. Start the timer.
4. At the first definite desire to breathe, stop the timer.

After the pause, you should be able to breathe effortlessly. If there is any desire to breathe faster or deeper, you have held on too long.

Understanding the exact moment of your control pause is difficult in the beginning. With practice, and as your control pause increases, it gets clearer.

If you would like more detailed descriptions of how to take your control pause, I found these pages useful:

- [How to measure your control pause](https://thebreathingman.com/control-pause/) by Vladimir and Marina Buteyko
- [How to measure your BOLT score](https://oxygenadvantage.com/measure-bolt/) by Patrick McKeown

## Very Shallow Breathing

Use shallow belly breathing to build and maintain a gentle sense of air hunger. Adjust the depth of your breath to maintain air hunger, while making sure that your belly stays relaxed.

Your breath should be relaxed, fluid and regular. The only control you exert is reducing the depth of your inhalation. Do not use any force or control for the exhalation, let your body exhale naturally.

If your belly becomes tense, you have generated too much air hunger. You can try briefly massaging your belly to release tension, or you can breathe slightly deeper to reduce air hunger.

If you lose air hunger, pause for a few seconds after exhalation until the sense of air hunger returns, and then resume shallow breathing.

## Recover

Allow your body to breathe naturally and let the sense of air hunger fade.

## Maximum Pauses

The basic process is the same as for the control pause. Only instead of releasing the breath hold at the first definite desire to breathe, you continue the hold for as long as you can.

At the end of a maximum pause, you will have a strong desire to breathe deeply. You must suppress the reflex to take a deep breath. Very rapid and shallow breathing can help suppress the desire.

The maximum pauses use a series of increasing distractions to help you extend your breath hold.

### Light Effort

While in an upright, seated position, hold your breath for as long as you can.

### Medium Effort

Same as the previous maximum pause. Before the urge to breathe becomes strong, add rocking and twisting movements to help extend the breath hold.

### Full Effort

Same as the previous maximum pause. After the rocking and twisting, and before the urge to breathe becomes strong, get up and walk around the room to help extend the breath hold.
